Business Context
TechStar Private Limited is a service-based company that manages client issues using the TechStar Support portal. Through this portal, clients can raise tickets, track their issue status, and communicate with the support team.
Behind the scenes, TechStar relies on the LENS Support system to manage and resolve these issues efficiently. Once a client raises a ticket, the delivery team creates tasks through issue and records the time and effort spent on them through timesheets
Problem Statement
During the settlement stage for an issue raised by one of TechStar’s key clients, ACCB, the finance team sent the final invoice for review. Soon after, an unexpected email landed in their inbox , the client claimed that the total effort mentioned in the invoice was higher than what had been mutually agreed upon.
What began as a simple clarification quickly turned into a long email thread that kept growing. As the discussion grew tense, the delivery head stepped in and requested the finance and delivery team to validate the accepted effort and compare with actutal effort 
The finance team found themselves juggling multiple tab, checking the timesheets, referring to task records in the issue, and cross-verifying approvals hidden deep within past email exchanges. Every small discrepancy led to another round of communication and confusion. What should have been a quick verification turned into hours of late-night effort and frustration across teams.
To address this recurring challenge, the TechStar finance team consulted with LMNAS Cloud Solutions, the provider of the LENS Support System. After reviewing the situation, the consultant observed that the confusion had not stemmed from miscommunication, but from the scattered nature of information effort approvals buried in emails instead of being recorded within the system, and task and timesheet details spread across different sections of LENS. Both the client and TechStar were, in essence, trying to piece together the same story from different fragments.
The consultant proposed that if this information could naturally flow into a single, visible view within the TechStar Support portal, it would not only prevent such misunderstandings but also build mutual trust and transparency between TechStar and its clients during future settlements. This consolidated view  accessible to both the TechStar team and their clients  would serve as a single source of truth, helping both parties validate the accepted and actual efforts with confidence.

    A food-delivery company uses LENS for taking their food orders. The kitchen flow works smoothly, and Delivery Notes are created in the LENS. The operational problems start only when the food is ready for delivery. 

Drivers depend on printed paper slips of Delivery Note to know which order to take. This leads to repeated problems: 
    • Slips get misplaced, torn, or lost. 
    • No one can confirm who picked an order first. 
    • No clear record of when a driver left for delivery. 
    • When customers ask for an update, the team cannot give an immediate or accurate answer.

To find the status of an order, staff must search through WhatsApp messages, call drivers one by one, and sort through unorganized paper slips. This creates delays, customer complaints, and internal frustration. Arguments between drivers and staff are frequent because there is no reliable tracking.

The company also provides delivery incentives. Orders under 2 km delivered within 30 minutes earn extra points. But since drivers record the times themselves, the data is inconsistent. Some report correctly, some forget, some guess, and some adjust times later. Weekly incentive reports rarely match reality, causing conflict between kitchen staff, drivers, and management. 

The company wants to improve this part of the workflow without replacing LENS. They want a simple Nextjs interface that drivers can use on their phones so delivery activities are tracked clearly and the confusions are solved.
